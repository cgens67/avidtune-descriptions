Released in 1972, this two-song EP came out at a time when Motown was doing everything it could to keep Michael Jackson’s solo career moving fast. Right after his first hit solo record, the label wanted to make sure listeners saw Michael as his own artist, not just the kid leading the Jackson 5.
To make that happen, Motown paired "Ben" (a song written for a movie about a killer rat) with "You Can Cry On My Shoulder," a sweet soul song written by Motown founder Berry Gordy.
At first, a lot of people in the music industry were skeptical. They saw the release as a cheap cash grab, thinking Motown was just using a movie tie-in to squeeze quick money out of Michael’s growing fame. People also worried Motown was over-saturating the market, throwing out too many Michael songs while the Jackson 5 were still actively touring and putting out hits.
However, those doubts faded once people actually listened to the music. Michael sang "Ben" with so much heart and feeling that people forgot all about the weird movie plot line. The song went straight to number one, giving Michael his first solo mega-hit, while the B-side offered a solid dose of classic Motown soul. What looked like a cash grab ended up proving just how talented Michael really was.
