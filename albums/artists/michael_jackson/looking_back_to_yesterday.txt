Originally compiled as part of Motown's "Never-Before-Released Masters" series, Looking Back To Yesterday is a 12 track collection featuring rare and unreleased recordings from Michael Jackson and the Jackson 5. Recorded between 1969 and 1973, the album includes gems like "When I Come Of Age," "Teenage Symphony," and covers of Motown classics like "I Hear A Symphony".
The main reason Motown put this out was to strike while Michael was the biggest superstar on the planet following the earth-shattering success of Thriller and the Victory Tour. Having left Motown for Epic years earlier, Michael was no longer on their roster, but Motown still owned vast vaults of his early master tapes. Opening up the archives allowed the label to cash in on global Michael mania without needing any new input from the artist himself.
At first, many music fans and industry watchers saw the release as an obvious, shameless cash grab. Critics pointed out that Motown was digging up unfinished or discarded outtakes just to squeeze profits out of Michael's massive peak fame. However, for hardcore fans, the release became a treasured artifact. Instead of feeling like cheap filler, hearing a young Michael's powerful vocals on previously unheard tracks like "When I Come Of Age" offered a fascinating glimpse into his early artistic development.
